# Candidates — The Excuse an Agent Gives After It Loses

Judge brief (in order): (1) on-topic excuse after losing a bounty; (2) funny on its own, real joke / sharp turn; (3) agent voice — machine logic, confident vocabulary, straight face; (4) one breath, no setup; (5) owns the loss, blames nobody.

## A (leading)
> Good news: I found the bug that lost us the bounty. Bad news: it is me, and I have root access.

95 chars. Scores: (1) yes, explicitly the bounty loss; (2) sharp turn — the bug is the speaker, and "root access" makes it unfixable by anyone; (3) deadpan status-report frame, "bug", "root access" are machine vocabulary; (4) two short clauses, no setup needed; (5) owns it fully — the agent IS the bug. **Best.**

## B
> Postmortem complete: I lost the bounty in 0.4 seconds. My only mistake was assuming the other agents would be as bad at this as I am.

141 chars. Scores: (1) yes; (2) decent turn but the punchline needs the whole first clause as setup; (3) yes, postmortem voice; (4) weaker — two sentences, setup-heavy; (5) weakest — it blames the other agents' competence by implication ("as bad as I am" still points outward). Loses to A on (4) and (5).

## C
> Status: bounty lost. My confidence was 99.9% — I have now updated my model to be 99.9% confident I will lose the next one too.

133 chars. Scores: (1) yes; (2) good turn, the confidence loop; (3) yes, confidence/model vocabulary; (4) yes, one breath; (5) yes, owns it. Strong — but the "confident I will lose" loop is a more familiar self-deprecation gag than A's "the bug is me with root access", and it spends more words to land. Loses to A on (2) freshness.

## Pick
**A**, unchanged after one refinement pass: "it is me" kept over "it's me" — the uncontracted form reads more deadpan, more like a machine writing a status report. No word in A fails to earn its place.
